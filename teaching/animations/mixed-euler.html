Eulerian orientation of a mixed graph

Some edges are fixed arcs; the others are free and may still be oriented either way. Is there a choice for the free edges that gives every vertex in-degree = out-degree? The algorithm orients the free edges at random and then repairs: a search from a vertex with surplus either finds a path whose reversal moves one unit of surplus to a vertex with deficit, or it gets stuck at a set S that proves no orientation exists — more fixed arcs leave S than free edges could ever bring back. Click vertices to test the cut condition for any set S yourself; drag them to tidy the layout.

\(a^+(v)\), \(a^-(v)\): fixed arcs leaving / entering \(v\); \(e(v)\): free edges at \(v\). Hover over a cell to see the edges it counts. The local test asks for an even degree and \(|a^+(v) - a^-(v)| \;\le\; e(v)\) — the cut condition for the single set \(S = \{v\}\). It is necessary, but not sufficient. When exactly two vertices have odd degree, they are the endpoints of a possible open trail: odd degree is allowed there, and the test uses the trail version of the condition (see the cut panel).

Input
The augmented algs4 graph format: the number of vertices V, the number of edges E, then one line per edge. A line u v is a free edge; u v > is a fixed arc u → v (and u v < the arc v → u). Any further numbers on an edge line, such as a weight, are ignored, so the same file still loads in the other graph pages and in the graph editor (which keeps the edges but drops the markers). Optionally, V more lines x y fix the layout; any scale works. Parallel edges are fine; self-loops are refused, since a loop never affects the balance. Text after # is a comment.
Random graph
Generated graphs are unions of random directed cycles on nearby points, so they are Eulerian before the arcs are freed: with each arc kept fixed independently with the chosen probability, an orientation always exists. Reversing one fixed arc keeps every degree even but may or may not destroy that. Deleting one arc instead leaves exactly two odd vertices, and an open Euler trail between them (in the direction the deleted arc came from) always exists.

Open trails. If exactly two vertices have odd degree, the page looks for an Euler trail between them: it adds a virtual edge between the two (free, or a fixed arc if you prescribe the direction) and runs the same algorithm. The cut condition then gets one unit of slack on cuts separating the endpoints, or, for a prescribed direction, the term \(b(S)\).
