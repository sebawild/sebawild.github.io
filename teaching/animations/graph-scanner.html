How it works

The photo is flattened (background normalisation per colour channel) and thresholded into an ink mask. Nodes are the circular holes of the most frequent large size (so letter loops and faces between edges are ignored). Straight edges are found by checking every pair of nodes for ink along the connecting segment, tolerating gaps at the ends (hand-drawn edges that stop short of a node) and gaps where a label sits on the line; segments passing through another node, or explained by a path through a third node, are rejected. Remaining long strokes are traced as curved edges and attached to the nearest nodes. Arrowheads are extra ink trailing backwards from an edge end. Labels and weights are read with Tesseract (printed text) and a small handwritten-digit network; indexed labels such as v1 take their letter from the majority of nodes.

Workflow

Items drawn dashed in orange are uncertain — tap to check them. Everything you change (moving, relabelling, adding, deleting) is kept when you tweak the recognition settings and re-scan (turning or cropping the photo starts afresh). Orientation: phones do not always store which way up a photo was taken. Nodes and edges do not care, but reading labels does, so after recognising the structure the largest glyphs are read in all four orientations (together with where indices such as the 1 in v1 sit) and the photo is turned the way it reads best. Use ⟲ / ⟳ to override; in Crop / straighten the thick edge marks the top. For best results: dark pen, circled nodes, arrowheads with a little gap before the node, weights written beside the edge, and a photo taken straight from above. If small nodes are missed, raise the working resolution.

Output

algs4: V, E, one v w [weight] line per edge, then one x y line per vertex, exactly as the graph editor writes it; vertices are numbered by their labels if they are distinct (otherwise in reading order), and the mapping is shown below the text. TikZ: named nodes, one line per edge, styles at the top (quotes, arrows.meta and shapes.geometric libraries). JSON: the full scan (graphscan/1) with geometry, arrow scores, OCR alternatives and your edits.
