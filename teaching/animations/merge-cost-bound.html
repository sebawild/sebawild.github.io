The merge-cost bound

Why Powersort and Peeksort merge with a cost of at most about \(n(\mathscr H + 2)\) — the proofs, animated. The merge cost as piles of runs. For Powersort, the intervals between run midpoints fall through sieves of ever finer grids, which bounds every pile. For Peeksort, each merge pays for itself, from an entropy budget and from credits of the runs.

The proofs, spelled out

Setting

The input consists of runs \(R_1, \ldots, R_r\) of lengths \(\ell_1, \ldots, \ell_r\), and \(n = \ell_1 + \cdots + \ell_r\). Both methods merge along a binary merge tree whose leaves are the runs; a merge costs the length of its result. With \(\operatorname{depth}(R_i)\), the number of merges that \(R_i\) takes part in, \[ M \;=\; \sum_{\text{merges } P} |P| \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{r} \ell_i \cdot \operatorname{depth}(R_i). \] The entropy of the run lengths is \(\mathscr H = \sum_i \frac{\ell_i}{n} \lg\frac{n}{\ell_i}\), so \(n\mathscr H = \sum_i \ell_i \lg\frac{n}{\ell_i}\).

Theorem 1 (Powersort): \(M \le n(\mathscr H + 2)\)

Powers. Let \(\mu_i\) be the middle of the run \(R_i\), as a position in \([0, n]\). The boundary between \(R_{i-1}\) and \(R_i\) (\(2 \le i \le r\)) has the midpoint interval \(I_i = (\mu_{i-1}, \mu_i]\) and the power \[ P_i \;=\; \min\bigl\{\, p \ge 1 \;:\; I_i \text{ contains a multiple of } n\,2^{-p} \,\bigr\}, \qquad P_1 = P_{r+1} = 0. \]

The tree. Powersort’s merge tree is the Cartesian tree of the powers: the root is the boundary of smallest power, and the two subtrees are built in the same way from the boundaries to its left and to its right. Within a part, the smallest power is attained only once. Two multiples of \(n\,2^{-p}\) in the intervals of two of its boundaries would enclose a multiple of \(n\,2^{-(p-1)}\), which lies in the interval of a boundary in between, with a smaller power. Hence powers strictly increase along every path down the tree. The ancestors of \(R_i\) are boundaries with distinct powers, from \(1\) up to the power of its parent, which is the larger of its two neighbouring boundaries: \[ \operatorname{depth}(R_i) \;\le\; \max\{P_i, P_{i+1}\}. \]

Long intervals have small powers. An interval \((a, b]\) of length at least \(n\,2^{-p}\) contains a multiple of \(n\,2^{-p}\), wherever it lies — this is what the moving sieve shows.

The intervals are long. \(I_i\) contains the left half of \(R_i\), and \(I_{i+1}\) its right half; both are of length \(\ell_i/2\). Let \(d_i\) be the level with \(2^{-d_i} \le \frac{\ell_i/2}{n} \lt 2^{-d_i+1}\), i.e. \(d_i = \lceil \lg(n/\ell_i) + 1 \rceil\). Then \(n\,2^{-d_i} \le \ell_i/2\), so \(P_i \le d_i\) and \(P_{i+1} \le d_i\), and \[ \operatorname{depth}(R_i) \;\le\; d_i \;\lt\; \lg\frac{n}{\ell_i} + 2. \]

The sum. \(\displaystyle M = \sum_i \ell_i \operatorname{depth}(R_i) \;\le\; \sum_i \ell_i \Bigl(\lg\frac{n}{\ell_i} + 2\Bigr) = n\mathscr H + 2n.\) ∎

Theorem 2 (Peeksort): \(M \le n\mathscr H + 2n - \ell_1 - \ell_r\)

Here a single run can be deeper than \(\lg(n/\ell_i) + 2\) — see the preset “a run deeper than its share” — so the bound has to be proved for the sum. For a part \(P\) of the merge tree (a range of consecutive runs, \(|P|\) elements) let \[ E(P) \;=\; \sum_{R \subseteq P} \ell_R \lg\frac{|P|}{\ell_R}, \] let \(M(P)\) be the cost of the merges inside \(P\), and \(\ell_{\mathrm{first}}(P)\), \(\ell_{\mathrm{last}}(P)\) the lengths of its first and its last run. \(h(x) = -x\lg x - (1-x)\lg(1-x)\) is the binary entropy.

Claim. For every part \(P\): \(\;M(P) \;\le\; E(P) + 2|P| - \ell_{\mathrm{first}}(P) - \ell_{\mathrm{last}}(P)\). For the whole input, \(E = n\mathscr H\), and this is the theorem.

Proof by induction over the tree. A single run has \(M = E = 0\) and \(2\ell - \ell - \ell = 0\). Now let \(P = A[\ell..r)\) be cut at \(g\) into \(P' = A[\ell..g)\) and \(P'' = A[g..r)\), and \(x = |P'|/|P|\). The merge costs \(|P|\), and by the chain rule of entropy \[ E(P) \;=\; E(P') + E(P'') + |P|\,h(x). \] The outer runs of \(P'\) and \(P''\) are those of \(P\), plus the two runs at the cut. So the induction hypothesis for both parts gives \[ M(P) \;=\; |P| + M(P') + M(P'') \;\le\; E(P) + 2|P| - \ell_{\mathrm{first}}(P) - \ell_{\mathrm{last}}(P) \;+\; \Bigl[\, |P|\bigl(1 - h(x)\bigr) - \ell_{\mathrm{last}}(P') - \ell_{\mathrm{first}}(P'') \,\Bigr], \] and it remains to show that the bracket is at most 0: the gap \(|P|(1 - h(x))\) is paid by the two runs at the cut.

Convexity. \(\varphi(\delta) = 1 - h(\tfrac12 + \delta)\) is convex on \([0, \tfrac12]\), because \(h\) is concave, with \(\varphi(0) = 0\) and \(\varphi(\tfrac12) = 1\). So it lies below its chord, \(1 - h(\tfrac12 + \delta) \le 2\delta\). With \(\delta = |x - \tfrac12|\), since \(h\) is symmetric, \[ |P|\bigl(1 - h(x)\bigr) \;\le\; 2\delta\,|P| \;=\; |2g - \ell - r|. \]

The cut is close to the middle. Peeksort takes \(m = \ell + \lfloor (r - \ell)/2 \rfloor\), the run \(R_k = A[i..j)\) with \(i \le m \lt j\), and cuts at \(g = i\) if \(m - i \lt j - m\), else at \(g = j\). Note \(\ell + r - 2m \in \{0, 1\}\). If \(g = i\): as \(m - i \lt j - m\) are integers, \(2(m - i) \le \ell_k - 1\), so \(|2g - \ell - r| = 2(m - i) + (\ell + r - 2m) \le \ell_k\). If \(g = j\): \(2(j - m) \le \ell_k\), so \(|2g - \ell - r| = 2(j - m) - (\ell + r - 2m) \le \ell_k\). Either way \[ |P|\bigl(1 - h(x)\bigr) \;\le\; \ell_k \;\le\; \ell_{\mathrm{last}}(P') + \ell_{\mathrm{first}}(P''), \] because the cut is at an end of \(R_k\): it is the last run of \(P'\) or the first run of \(P''\). ∎

As in the animation. Unrolling the induction, \[ M \;=\; \sum_P |P| \;=\; \sum_P |P|\,h(x_P) \;+\; \sum_P |P|\bigl(1 - h(x_P)\bigr) \;=\; n\mathscr H + \sum_P \mathrm{gap}_P : \] the budgets (green) add up to \(n\mathscr H\) exactly. Each gap is at most the length of the run \(R_k\) at its cut — that run’s credit for this boundary (gold). Each boundary is cut exactly once, and each run has two boundaries, except the two outer runs, which have one each. So \(\sum_P \mathrm{gap}_P \le 2n - \ell_1 - \ell_r\).

Why not the same proof for both? The local inequality fails for Powersort. Its cuts lie next to a point of the global grid, which can be far from the middle of the current part. Powersort’s proof uses the global grid instead, and then works run by run.

Input as text
The elements, separated by spaces, commas or line breaks — the format of the algs4 sorting clients. Only the runs matter here: the maximal increasing stretches. If every element is a number they are compared numerically, otherwise as strings. Lines starting with # are comments.

Instead of the elements, a line @runs 5 3 3 14 1 2 asks for an input with exactly these run lengths, and @seed 3 picks another such input.

At most 256 elements. The same works in the page address, e.g. #runs=26,23,3,19,1&peek (&peek for Peeksort, Powersort otherwise; &present for the presentation view).
