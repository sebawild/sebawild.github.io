Rooting Out EntropyOptimal Tree Extraction for Ultra‑Succinct Graphs

Ziad Ismaili Alaoui · Liverpool & Marburg    Tamio‑Vesa Nakajima · Marburg    Namrata · Birmingham    Julius Reitlinger · Marburg    Sebastian Wild · Marburg & Liverpool

A graph stored as adjacency lists pays for vertex names it does not need. We let a spanning tree choose the names: extract the tree, store it in 2 bits per edge, and keep only what is left in an entropy‑compressed string. The result is a drop‑in replacement for adjacency lists using bits, with all navigational queries in time — and a new optimisation problem: which tree?

Tree extraction removes edges and adds bits, so its relative effect is governed by the average degree : sparse road and power grids shrink by two thirds, dense social networks by a few percent.

In units of (middle), the saving is remarkably stable: the greedy tree recovers essentially the full label information, whatever the density.

Two toy graphs with identical degree sequences mark the extremes: a path with two hubs saves 57 % over the wavelet tree; three stars sharing their leaves save 3 % — their entropy was already constant.
