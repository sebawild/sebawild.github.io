About this page — conventions and what to look for

Two separate stages. The switch next to the title selects between failureLinks(P), which looks at the pattern only, and the matching loop, which uses the finished links. Both pseudocodes are in the code panel; the active one has its heading in red, the other one is greyed out.

Colours. Red dashed edges are failure links, green marks the failure state x during the construction together with the edge it follows, and red marks the position currently being processed.

Stage 1. The strip shows j in red and, underneath, the prefix P[0..x) placed where it occurs as a suffix of P[1..j) — the invariant "x is the state reached after reading P[1..j)". The comparison counter illustrates the amortisation: the while loop only ever decreases x, and x increases at most once per iteration of the for loop, so at most 2m comparisons overall.

Stage 2. One row per guess. Characters in parentheses are known to match because of the failure link and are never compared again; × marks a mismatch. The bottom row lists the states passed after reading each character, so following several failure links at one text position shows up as several values in one cell.

Why it is fast. The two bars are the text position i and the guess i − q. Neither ever decreases, each iteration pushes at least one of them up by one (a mismatch in state 0 pushes both), and neither can pass n. Hence at most 2n iterations and at most 2n character comparisons. The chip i + (i−q) shows this potential directly.

A note on the two arrays. The failure links used here coincide with the KMP prefix function: fail[q] = Π(q) is the length of the longest proper border of P[0..q). The loop as printed fills fail[0..m); fail[m], which the good-suffix computation of Boyer-Moore needs, is the value of x after the loop.
