Optimal alphabetic trees

Given leaf weights ℓ0,…,ℓn, find the binary search tree whose null pointers L0,…,Ln minimise c(T) = Σ ℓi·depth(Li). The guess is the root r, which splits the leaves into [i..r) and [r..j); the subproblems are the ranges. Every leaf in a subtree pays 1 at its root, so a range costs its total weight plus what the two halves cost. Hover a cell for its range; click one to build the optimal tree for it alone.

The same DP, but the root is not guessed from the whole range. Write R(i,j) for an optimal root of [i..j). Empirically — and provably, via the quadrangle inequality — the optimal root never moves backwards when the range grows: R(i,j−1) ≤ R(i,j) ≤ R(i+1,j). Restricting the r-loop to that window costs nothing and turns Θ(n³) into Θ(n²) — the windows along one diagonal telescope. The blue band in each cell is the window actually searched.

The tree
Merge trees compared: optimal vs Peeksort vs Powersort
Read as a merge tree for run-adaptive sorting: the leaves are runs, every internal node is one merge, and a merge costs the total length of what it merges. So the merge cost of the whole sort is Σ over internal nodes of the subtree weight — exactly c(T) again, and the shaded area of the drawing is the cost. Peeksort and Powersort never solve a DP, yet both stay within the entropy bounds H ≤ c(T*) and c ≤ H+2. Neither is optimal in general.

Input
One line of positive numbers: the leaf weights ℓ₀ ℓ₁ … ℓₙ, whitespace or comma separated. Lines starting with # are ignored. They need not sum to 1 — everything is normalised, and both the raw values and the normalised ones are shown. In the merge-tree reading they are run lengths, so integers are the natural input there. Between 2 and 12 weights.
