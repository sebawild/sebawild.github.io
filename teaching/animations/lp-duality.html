Dual linear programs

How good can a solution of an LP be? Every constraint is a true statement about every feasible point; so is every sum of multiples of them. Choose a multiplier for each constraint and add up: if the sum compares with the objective column by column, it bounds the optimum — no solving needed.

The best multipliers: the dual LP
Weak duality, signs, strong duality

Take any feasible solution \(\mathbf x\) of the LP and any feasible solution \(\mathbf y\) of its dual. Their values differ by a sum of products, one per constraint and one per variable; each product is \(\ge 0\). Both are optimal exactly when all products are \(0\): a multiplier is positive only on a tight constraint, a variable only where its dual condition is tight.
